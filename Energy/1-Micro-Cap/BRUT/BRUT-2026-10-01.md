## 2. Five-Pillar Breakdown

### Pillar 1: Valuation (**0/6**)

**Method 1: ship-by-ship equity cash flows over 15 years.**

For each ship, the model takes charter income minus operating costs (~$10.5k/day) and G&A (~$1k/day), minus the fixed lease payment (~$10.5M a year per LNG ship, ~$9.0M per conventional ship). At year 15 it adds the ship's resale value minus the remaining lease balance. Fixed charters are honoured first, then a market-rate assumption applies.

|---|---|---|---|
| VLCC rates ($k/day): first year / 2nd / 3rd / long-term | 60 / 45 / 38 / 35 | **85 / 62 / 50 / 45** | 115 / 90 / 70 / 55 |
| Cost of equity | 13% | **11%** | 10% |
| Equity value | US$12M | **US$128M** | US$279M |
| **Value per share** | **NOK 1.9** | **NOK 20.6** | **NOK 45.0** |

The probability-weighted value (30% bear, 45% base, 25% bull) is **NOK 21.1, or −56%**.

The base case's long-term $45k/day is roughly the historical average for a modern VLCC. **Even the bull case, which assumes rates stay well above average for years, is just below the price.**

**Method 2: mark-to-market NAV (broker-style).**

| Step | US$M |
|---|---|
| Charter-free ship values: ~$180M × 2 (prompt dual-fuel) + ~$160M × 2 (2027 slots) | 680 |
| Less full lease debt | −462 |
| Less remaining yard equity *(est.)* | −30 |
| Plus corporate cash *(est.)* | +15 |
| **NAV** | **≈ 203 (NOK 32.7 per share)** |

With slightly higher ship values (~$190M and ~$172M), NAV is about **NOK 45**. So the stock trades at about **1.1–1.5x NAV on today's near-record ship prices**. With ~90% leverage, every 15% fall in ship values cuts NAV by about **NOK 16 per share**. At 70% of today's values, NAV is roughly zero.

**Reading the two methods together:** BRUT is priced at or above the value of its ships *at today's peak prices*. Its long-run cash-flow value is far lower. **The equity behaves like a leveraged option on VLCC rates staying high.**

## 4. Catalysts, Risks & Red Flags

**Growth drivers**

1. **Strong rates with contracted cash flow.**
   - Two of the four ships are fixed at about $95–106k/day, against a $35–39k/day breakeven.
   - One-year time-charter rates are about $135k/day, so ships 3 and 4 could be fixed at six-figure rates before delivery.
   - Monthly distributions are likely to rise as ships deliver.
2. **Asset prices and a proven seller.**
   - Ships available sooner are selling at $178–200M, against Bruton's ~$122–138M contract prices.
   - Trøim (of Frontline, Golar and Seadrill fame) has a record of crystallising value: the OMC spin-off is an example. Selling one ship or yard slot would lock in about US$40–60M per ship.
3. **Structural tanker tailwinds.**
   - The global VLCC fleet is ageing, and sanctions have split the market, with a "shadow fleet" taking up ships.
   - Trade routes are longer, so each voyage ties up ships for longer.
   - Shipyards are full until about 2029.
   - Older ships recycle into the shadow fleet rather than competing for mainstream cargoes.

**Risks and red flags**

1. **Rates are at a cyclical peak.**
   - One-year rates and second-hand prices are at multi-decade highs.
   - The newbuild orderbook is surging, with deliveries clustered in 2027–29, the same years as OMC's ships.
   - A sanctions unwind (Russia, Iran or Venezuela) or an OPEC+ output cut could take $40–60k/day off rates quickly.
   - At $45k long-term, the equity is worth about NOK 21.
2. **Leverage of 85–90%.**
   - Equity is a thin slice: a 15% fall in ship values removes about a third of NAV, and a 30% fall removes all of it.
   - Lease structures also restrict selling ships.
3. **No track record, a small fleet and information gaps.**
   - With four ships, a single off-hire or charterer problem matters.
   - Mount Vision's index-linked charter after April 2027 depends on the counterparty.
   - Post-spin cash and book equity were not disclosed in what I could retrieve.
   - Broker targets appear stale (pre-spin).
   - The stock trades on Euronext Growth with limited liquidity.
