## 4. Catalysts, Risks & Red Flags

**Growth drivers**

1. **Fast-growing maritime LEO adoption.**
   - Vessels added more than 1,000 net in Q2 (+11% QoQ), and airtime grew 31%.
   - Crew-welfare rules, digital operations and cheaper bandwidth are pushing commercial fleets onto Starlink and OneWeb.
   - KVH's install base, global service network and hybrid CommBox give it a role as a fleet-management partner.
2. **Operating leverage plus cash returns.**
   - The adjusted EBITDA guide of $11–16M implies a stronger H2 (H1 was $5.8M).
   - Each extra $10M of airtime at about 36% gross margin adds about $3.6M of gross profit against a mostly fixed cost base.
   - Net cash is 42% of the market cap, the buyback is close to finished, and there have been property sales.
3. **Possible takeover target.**
   - Maritime connectivity is consolidating (Marlink, Speedcast, Viasat-Inmarsat, Anuvu).
   - At about 6x EBITDA with a clean balance sheet and 10,700 vessels, KVH would be a cheap way for a larger provider to add subscribers.

**Risks and red flags**

1. **Supplier power: SpaceX sets the economics.**
   - Starlink sells maritime plans directly and through many resellers.
   - Any pricing change or move to cut out intermediaries would squeeze KVH's margins. This is my bear case ($3.87).
   - The legacy VSAT airtime base is shrinking.
2. **Thin profitability.**
   - The Q2 net margin was about 0.6%, and Q2 **missed profit expectations**, so the shares fell.
   - Adjusted EBITDA flatters GAAP: depreciation on leased terminals is a real cost.
   - The TTM GAAP result is negative, and the company has a history of restructurings and impairments.
3. **Scale and liquidity.**
   - It is a ~$138M company competing with much larger integrators, with limited analyst coverage and trading.
   - The wide guidance range (adjusted EBITDA $11–16M) signals low visibility.
   - It depends on the maritime industry's appetite to spend.
