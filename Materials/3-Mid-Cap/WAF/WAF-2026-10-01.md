## 4. Catalysts, Risks & Red Flags

**Growth drivers**

1. **Cash returns to shareholders.**
   - Pro-forma net cash is about A$0.5B including bullion, and annual attributable FCF is about A$650M at US$4,300 gold, a ~16% yield.
   - Debt prepayment is underway, and WAF has paid its first dividend.
   - About 15c per share more is coming from the Kiaka sale proceeds.
   - Management is discussing a recurring capital-management framework (dividends or buybacks).
2. **Volume growth with long life.**
   - Kiaka is still ramping (67.6 koz in Q2, constrained by explosives permits).
   - Toega's first open-pit gold arrives in Q3-26, and the M5 underground follows.
   - Production heads to about 596 koz in 2030, from a 7.0 Moz reserve and a 13.7 Moz resource. Drilling at M5 shows extensions below reserves.
3. **Gold price leverage and a valuation re-rating.**
   - Each US$100/oz of gold adds about A$31M a year of attributable FCF.
   - At 0.72x NAV and ~6x P/E, any sign that the state relationship is stabilising, such as completing the Kiaka deal on fair terms, could narrow the discount.

**Risks and red flags**

1. **Sovereign and expropriation risk in Burkina Faso. This is the dominant risk.**
   - The military government nationalised five gold assets from Endeavour and Lilium in 2024–25, paying just US$60M plus a royalty, and its PM said in 2025 that more nationalisations would follow.
   - The state is now taking an **extra 25% of Kiaka for about A$175M**. That values 100% of Kiaka at about A$700M, a fraction of its NAV, so it is a material forced value transfer.
   - A similar move on Sanbrado is my bear case. Full nationalisation is my 15% tail case.
   - There is also jihadist insurgency risk to logistics and staff.
2. **Gold price.**
   - Gold has already pulled back from the ~US$4,744 H1 average to about US$4,300.
   - At US$3,300 the base NAV falls to about A$3.18 per share, or below the share price in the bear case.
   - Royalties and AISC rise with price, so margins compress less, but rise less, than spot.
3. **Operational and permit friction.**
   - Kiaka's explosives-facility permit was not approved, which constrained mining in Q2.
   - Sanbrado's updated life-of-mine approval is still pending.
   - Kiaka is lower-grade (~0.9 g/t), so it is sensitive to costs.
   - Margins are already compressing: AISC was US$1,488 in 2025 and is guided below US$1,900 for 2026.
